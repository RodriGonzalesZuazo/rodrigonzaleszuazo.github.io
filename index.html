I am an environmental economist at Conservation Strategy Fund in Santa Cruz, Bolivia. My research seeks to understand the economic drivers of tropical deforestation and how forest loss feeds back into the economy, through econometric and structural modeling combined with satellite land-cover data, with Bolivia and the Amazon as empirical settings.

Before CSF, I was Principal Economist for the Real Sector at Bolivia's Ministry of Economy and Public Finance, where I worked on macroeconomic policy design with the Central Bank of Bolivia. I have also worked on biodiversity and financial sustainability with international organizations, including the IDB and UNDP. I hold an MA from Brandeis University, which I attended as a Joint Japan/World Bank Graduate Scholar, one of two Latin Americans selected in a global cohort of more than 120.

Research

Working papers

Productivity, Deforestation, and the Extensive Trap: A Structural Macro Model for Bolivia

Presented at the Conference on Nature and the Macroeconomy, Central Bank of Chile – CETEx (LSE) – SUREAL (U. Zurich), Santiago de Chile, 2026

Abstract

This paper develops D-ESTEEM, a structural macro-ecological model of the Bolivian economy, to study how primary forest loss feeds back into productivity and the external sector. Deforestation expands agricultural land while eroding the ecosystem services that sustain yields, pushing the economy into an extensive trap in which growth relies on further land conversion rather than productivity gains. The key ecological elasticity is micro-founded with a municipal panel from Santa Cruz combining satellite land-cover data and panel econometrics. Policy scenarios trace the macroeconomic and fiscal consequences of alternative land-use paths.

The Relationship Between Deforestation and Productivity in Farmlands in Bolivia

Mimeo prepared for Fundación FAUTAPO

Abstract

Using a panel of 56 municipalities in Santa Cruz over 2013–2024, this study estimates the causal effect of deforestation on agricultural productivity. It combines MapBiomas land-cover data with two-way fixed effects and Mundlak decompositions, validated against satellite vegetation indices. The results document significant yield losses associated with forest loss, challenging the assumption that agricultural expansion into forest land is productivity-enhancing.

Cultural Consensus on Environmental Conservation Programs: Mechanisms Behind a Randomized Controlled Trial in Rural Bolivia

Master's thesis, Brandeis University, 2025

Abstract

This thesis studies the mechanisms behind a conservation-payment randomized controlled trial (Watershared) in the Bolivian Andes. Drawing on original household survey data, it applies Cultural Consensus Analysis to measure how communities understand conservation agreements and how that shared understanding relates to program outcomes.
